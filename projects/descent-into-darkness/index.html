In Development

About the Game

Descent Into Darkness is an old-school dungeon crawler reforged for a modern audience — a grim underground fantasy of torchlit corridors, cursed ruins, and the relentless arithmetic of risk and reward. Assemble a party, descend into the dark, and decide with every room whether to press deeper toward greater spoils or extract before the dungeon claims everything you carry.

Built on tabletop-inspired fantasy systems and high-risk loot survival, every expedition is a wager. Death is heavy. Greed is heavier.

The Long Road Down

Design Direction

Descent Into Darkness is being shaped around dangerous dungeon runs where exploration matters as much as combat. The long-term goal is to make each descent feel uncertain — modular dungeon layouts, shifting room routes, valuable monster encounters, hidden lore, puzzle spaces, and extraction pressure forcing players to decide when to push deeper or leave alive.

The direction below describes where the project is headed. Much of it is still being prototyped, tested, and refined — treat it as intent, not a promise.

Direction 01

Procedural Dungeon Layouts

Future dungeon layouts are planned around modular room generation, where familiar room pieces can appear in different arrangements between runs. The goal is to reduce pure memorization and make exploration, navigation, and decision-making matter.

Direction 02

Organic Player Encounters

The goal is not instant chaos from spawn. The dungeon should create tension through exploration first, with player encounters happening naturally as groups move through routes, objectives, loot rooms, and extraction pressure.

Direction 03

Spawn Distance & Route Safety

Spawn placement and dungeon scale will be studied carefully so players have room to explore before conflict begins. The plan is to test distance rules, route separation, and dungeon size against lobby size.

Direction 04

PvE Needs to Matter

Monsters should not feel like filler. Creature encounters should provide meaningful danger and meaningful rewards, making PvE a real part of the dungeon economy instead of an obstacle players ignore.

Direction 05

Inventory Pressure & Backpacks

Inventory space should be part of the risk/reward loop. Future systems may include backpack progression, larger carrying options, crafted packs, rare storage items, and meaningful choices about what loot is worth keeping.

Direction 06

Exploration, Lore & Puzzles

The dungeon should reward more than fighting. Planned exploration features include lore fragments, hidden rooms, environmental puzzles, strange discoveries, and reasons to slow down instead of sprinting from fight to fight.

Direction 07

Darkness & Readability

Lighting should support fear, navigation, and tactical choices. The visual direction should preserve darkness where it matters while still keeping combat and interaction readable.

Direction 08

Multiple Run Styles Experimental

The project may support different dungeon experiences over time, such as larger exploration-focused runs and smaller combat-focused tests. These modes are still experimental and will depend on testing.
